# PayPal

> PayPal is a global payments company. Consumers use the PayPal app and wallet to pay online and in store, send money, pay later, save, and hold crypto. Businesses use PayPal to accept payments, run checkout, send payouts, manage risk, and access financing. Developers integrate through the APIs and SDKs documented at developer.paypal.com.

Key facts:
- PayPal offers Personal accounts (send, spend, and receive personal payments) and Business accounts (accept payments online, in store, and on the go, plus send invoices). Sign-up starts at the account selection page in the Consumers section.
- Pay Later offers two products in the US: Pay in 4 (short-term installments) and Pay Monthly (longer-term financing). Eligibility and terms vary by purchase and applicant.
- Purchase Protection may refund eligible purchases (full price plus original shipping) when an item is not received or is significantly not as described. Claims start in the Resolution Center. Exclusions apply; see the program terms.
- Seller Protection covers eligible Item Not Received and unauthorized transaction claims for sellers whose primary account address is in the US. For Item Not Received claims, sellers must ship to the address on the Transaction Details page and provide proof of shipment or proof of delivery.
- Fees differ for consumers and merchants. Sending money to friends and family from a bank account or PayPal balance is generally free domestically in the US; cards, currency conversion, and international transfers can carry fees. See the fee pages.
- Fastlane is PayPal's guest checkout: guest shoppers save their details once, then are recognized and autofilled at future checkouts without a password.
- Venmo is a separate PayPal service with its own terms and privacy statement. Merchants can accept Venmo through PayPal checkout in the US.
